## 1. Positioning Deep Dive (Dunford Method)

### The 5-Step Process

April Dunford's "Obviously Awesome" framework works backwards from what makes you different:

**Step 1: Competitive Alternatives**
Don't start with "what we do." Start with "what would customers do without us?"
- Include manual processes, spreadsheets, "do nothing"
- Include indirect competitors (different solution, same job)
- Talk to recent customers: "What were you using before us?"

**Step 2: Unique Attributes**
What can you do that alternatives genuinely can't?
- Technical capabilities (speed, integration, accuracy)
- Business model (pricing, support, flexibility)
- Domain expertise (industry knowledge, pre-built templates)

**Step 3: Value**
Each attribute enables specific value. Map them:
- Feature → Capability → Benefit → Business outcome
- Example: "Real-time sync (feature) → always-current data (capability) → fewer errors (benefit) → 20% less rework (outcome)"

**Step 4: Target Segment**
Who cares most about your unique value?
- Best-fit customers share characteristics that make your unique value crucial
- Segment by: company size, industry, tech stack, team structure, pain urgency
- The tighter your segment, the stronger your positioning

**Step 5: Market Category**
The frame of reference that makes your value obvious:
- Existing category leader? Position as "better at X"
- New subcategory? "Category + differentiator" (e.g., "conversational CRM")
- New category? Only if you have the budget to educate the market

### Match vs Ignore Framework

For every competitor feature:
| Decision | When | Example |
|----------|------|---------|
| **Match** | Table stakes — customers expect it | Basic reporting, SSO |
| **Ignore** | Not valued by your target segment | Enterprise features for SMB product |
| **Differentiate** | Your unique strength area | Invest and emphasize |

## 2. PR/FAQ Best Practices

### Writing the Press Release
- Write it as if the product is done and wildly successful
- If the press release is boring, the product idea isn't compelling enough
- Customer quotes should describe the transformation, not the features
- The internal FAQ is where you get honest about risks and costs

### Common Mistakes
- Too feature-focused: "Product X now supports Y" → should be about customer outcome
- Too vague: "Revolutionary new approach" → be specific about what changes
- Missing the "why now": every PR/FAQ needs a reason this matters today

## 3. Opportunity Solution Trees (Teresa Torres)

### Building an Effective OST
1. Start with a clear, measurable desired outcome (not a solution)
2. Discover opportunities through research (not brainstorming)
3. Each opportunity should come from real user evidence
4. Solutions are cheap to generate — opportunities are expensive to discover
5. Test assumptions before building solutions

### Assumption Mapping
For each solution, identify:
- **Desirability assumptions**: Will users want this?
- **Viability assumptions**: Will this work for the business?
- **Feasibility assumptions**: Can we build this?
- **Usability assumptions**: Can users figure it out?

Test the riskiest assumption first.

## 4. Vision Alignment Patterns

### Vision Levels
| Level | Timeframe | Audience | Detail |
|-------|-----------|----------|--------|
| Company vision | 10+ years | Everyone | Aspirational, one sentence |
| Product vision | 3-5 years | Product + leadership | From → To narrative |
| Product strategy | 1-2 years | Product + engineering | Bets, themes, sequencing |
| Roadmap | 1-4 quarters | All stakeholders | Features, timelines, outcomes |

### Alignment Test
After writing a vision, every team member should be able to answer:
1. Who are we building for?
2. What problem are we solving?
3. What does success look like?
4. What are we NOT doing?

If answers diverge, the vision isn't clear enough.

## 5. Portfolio Investment Framework

A common heuristic for balancing investment:

| Category | % of Resources | Purpose | Risk Profile |
|----------|---------------|---------|-------------|
| Core | ~70% | Optimize existing product for existing customers | Low risk, predictable returns |
| Adjacent | ~20% | Expand into related markets or capabilities | Medium risk, growth potential |
| Transformational | ~10% | Explore new markets or business models | High risk, breakthrough potential |

Adapt percentages to your strategy and stage:
- Early-stage startups: 80% core, 15% adjacent, 5% transformational
- Growth-stage: 60% core, 25% adjacent, 15% transformational
- Mature products: 50% core, 30% adjacent, 20% transformational
